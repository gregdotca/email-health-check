What it proves

DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance) ties SPF and DKIM to the domain people actually see: the one in the From: address. The domain publishes a policy in a DNS TXT record at _dmarc.domain, for example:

v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com

rua= is where receivers send DMARC reports: daily summaries (XML files) of the mail they saw from the domain and whether it passed. They're separate from this health check's report, and the best way to find mail that would fail before tightening the policy. The health check doesn't check that reports actually reach the rua= address.

A message passes DMARC when SPF or DKIM passes for a domain that matches the From: domain (see Alignment). The policy tells receivers what to do with mail that fails:

{% for policy in ("none", "quarantine", "reject") %} {% endfor %}
PolicyEffect on failing mail
p={{ policy }}{{ explain.dmarc_policy[policy] }}

pct= asks receivers to apply the policy to only that percentage of failing mail (100 if it's left out). It changes what happens to failures, not whether a message passes.

When the From: domain is a subdomain without its own record, the record of its organizational domain applies (with its sp= policy, if it has one).

ResultMeaning

"Not checked" means the message didn't have exactly one From: address. A domain with more than one DMARC record gets permerror: receivers ignore them all. Only a TXT record starting with v=DMARC1 counts, so a record with a misspelled or misplaced version tag is ignored (for a subdomain, its organizational domain's record may then apply).

Alignment

Alignment means the domain that passed SPF or DKIM matches the From: domain:

  • SPF aligned: SPF passed, and the envelope sender's domain matches the From: domain.
  • DKIM aligned: a DKIM signature passed, and the domain it's for matches the From: domain.

By default the match is relaxed: subdomains count, so bounces.example.com matches example.com. A record can ask for a strict (exact) match with aspf=s or adkim=s.

This is why a mail service that passes SPF and DKIM with its own domain can still fail DMARC for yours. Setting up DKIM for your own domain with the service is usually the fix.

Problem notes

A tag given twice (two p=, say) makes the record invalid: permerror. Other mistakes in the record get a "Problem:" note: an invalid or missing p= policy, an invalid sp=, an adkim= or aspf= value other than r or s, or a pct= that isn't a number from 0 to 100. The note says what receivers do instead. For example, a record with an invalid policy but a valid rua= reporting address is treated as p=none.

A "Problem:" note makes the summary's "Is authenticated" line {{ "warn"|mark }} (unless SPF has already made it {{ false|mark }}). So does DMARC that fails, and DMARC that's set up for mail with no DKIM signature: DMARC then depends on SPF alone, which breaks when mail is forwarded.

Fixing it

  • fail: make SPF or, better, DKIM pass for your own domain, for every service that sends your mail (see SPF and DKIM). Before tightening the policy, use the DMARC reports to check that all your real mail passes.
  • none: publish a record, starting with v=DMARC1; p=none; and a rua= address to receive reports. Move to quarantine or reject once the reports show all your mail passing.
  • permerror: fix the record so it starts with v=DMARC1 and has a valid p=, and make sure there's only one record at _dmarc.domain.
